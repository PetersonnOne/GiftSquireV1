# Story-led product demo script doctrine

Approved direction, 27 October 2026. Reusable across applications; adapt the product facts and proof.

## Purpose
Sell the value through a coherent, observable product story. Rich means meaningful and revealing, not crowded. Explain why actions matter rather than reading button labels.

## Narrative structure
1. Open with a short, emotionally engaging advertising-style question or situation grounded in the human problem. Keep this tone mainly in the opening.
2. Introduce the product and its distinctive benefit. Follow one consistent fictional example from need to outcome.
3. Show the manual workflow first: visible pointer, deliberate clicks, live character-by-character typing, legible fields and enough time to understand the outcome.
4. Make the essential technology's causal contribution inspectable: inputs, retrieved evidence, application decisions and result. Credit the actual responsibility of each system.
5. Transition naturally to voice as another way to complete the workflow. Let the assistant welcome first, the user speak, then review and confirm before changes.
6. Show text feedback then spoken feedback, and the resulting saved improvement. Demonstrate the whole loop rather than a feature menu.
7. Close with the human payoff, memorable product promise, and explicit thanks to the central partner.

## Writing and pacing
Fit the full piece under the agreed runtime, including dialogue, silence, transitions and end hold. Aim below the ceiling, with natural speaking rhythm. Reserve visual breathing room. Prefer concise concrete language, warm delivery, useful contrasts only when they help the story, and one clear takeaway per beat. Show strong verified examples. Never turn proposed outcomes into testimonials or clinical claims.

## Performance and sound
Keep narrator, user and assistant recognizably distinct. Preserve original speech stems and editable scripts. Express emotion through phrasing and restrained delivery direction. Do not force voices into a fixed duration with speed changes or time stretching. Audition for intelligibility, warmth and pronunciation; review the actual exported duration.

## Rehearsal versus final footage
An audio-only scripted dialogue is a planning reference, not evidence of an actual application response. Keep generation provenance and real-run acceptance in production notes. No intrusive synthesis labels or spoken caveats are required in the creative track. Never substitute scripted responses for proof of a live feature, or portray the actor as an independent customer testimonial. For a real application capture, play the prepared user lines into the microphone input and record the application's actual answers and saved result.

## Review checklist
Does the opening create interest? Is the product benefit immediate? Can a new viewer follow the manual steps? Is the central engine visibly essential? Are voice and feedback useful, understandable and confirmed? Does the saved result support the narration? Is the close memorable? Is the final runtime below the ceiling? Has a person listened and approved the performance?

## Reuse template
Audience / human problem / product promise / one example / manual journey / engine evidence / spoken alternative / improvement loop / saved payoff / partner credit / runtime / voice roles / evidence links / next review.

## Optional musical bed
Use sparse, warm instrumental texture without vocals, humming, a driving beat or dramatic swells. Keep speech foregrounded, duck music further during dialogue, and preserve the clean voice master. Audition a short combined sample before extending it to the whole film. Keep source, composition and mix provenance in backstage records; approve sound by listening rather than technical measurements alone.
